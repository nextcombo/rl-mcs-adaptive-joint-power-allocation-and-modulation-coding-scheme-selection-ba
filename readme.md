# [RL+MCS] Adaptive Joint Power Allocation and Modulation Coding Scheme Selection Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning for Energy Efficiency Links [with MATLAB Code]

Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Joint Power Allocation and Modulation Coding Scheme Selection in the Efficiency Link
Abstract: Link adaptation is an effective method for modern networks to cope with the time-varying characteristics of channels. Modulation coding scheme (MCS) selection is a core way to achieve dynamic channel adaptation, but next-generation networks require more flexible link adaptation schemes that integrate multiple parameters to enhance network performance. This paper proposes a high-efficiency link adaptation algorithm that introduces deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agents to achieve optimal matching between channel conditions and link parameters; in addition to the number of modulation levels and encoding rates, the downlink transmission power is also considered as part of the link parameter assessment, making the link adaptation more flexible and efficient. Simulation results show that this algorithm performs better than baseline algorithms in terms of energy efficiency and link throughput.
Introduction: Link adaptation is a core concept in modern high-speed communication networks such as 5G and beyond (B5G), which are essential due to two main aspects: First, to provide users with high-quality service (QoS) and experience quality (QoE), the communication network needs to adapt transmission technologies like modulation and coding to accommodate channel fluctuations, maintaining network connectivity and ensuring QoS; second, limited network resources such as bandwidth and energy necessitate balancing between QoS, QoE, connection maintenance, spectrum efficiency, and energy efficiency.
Modulation coding scheme (MCS) selection is the most commonly used method for link adaptation, where senders can choose different modulation techniques and encoding rates based on channel conditions: when channel conditions are good, higher order modulation and lower encoding rate are selected to improve resource efficiency; when channel conditions deteriorate, reducing the modulation order and increasing the encoding rate are employed to maintain connectivity, relying on the feedback from the receiver via Channel Quality Indicator (CQI) through Channel State Information (CSI). The Adaptive Modulation and Coding (AMC) specification for 5G New Radio (NR) defines the mapping process between CQI and MCS, but this static mapping fails to adapt to dynamic channels and cannot guarantee target block error rate (BLER).
In recent years, artificial intelligence and machine learning have been widely applied in 5G and B5G networks. Deep reinforcement learning, which combines deep learning, has learned optimal strategies from the practice of dynamic MCS selection, becoming an effective means to solve the problem of dynamic MCS selection. At the same time, influenced by economic, operational, and environmental factors, energy consumption optimization has become an important goal in network design. The number of connected devices in 5G and B5G networks is enormous, urgently requiring energy efficiency to be incorporated into the optimization dimension of link adaptation.
Traditional link adaptation methods only consider MCS schemes, which struggle to adapt to the high flexibility of 5G NR. It is necessary to upgrade to a multi-domain link adaptation method that incorporates more parameters. However, increasing parameters will expand the decision space, making the solution for the optimal solution more complex. Machine learning is an ideal technology to address this challenge. Based on this background, this paper proposes an intelligent energy-efficient MCS selection algorithm based on deep reinforcement learning. This algorithm jointly optimizes MCS and transmission power to maximize energy efficiency while considering throughput. The computational overhead of the algorithm is borne by the base station with unlimited computing capability, without affecting terminal performance.
1  
System Model: Based on the 5G NR architecture, focusing on the physical downlink shared channel (PDSCH) data transmission between base station (gNB) and user equipment (UE). The gNB sends Channel State Information Reference Signals (CSIRS), and the UE detects and feedbacks CQI (integer from 1 to 15, higher the number, better the channel condition). Considering actual channel characteristics such as feedback delay, channel fading, and noise, and assuming that the feedback channel is error-free and the downlink channel is free of interference.
2  
DRL Foundation Framework: Integrating Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Deep Neural Networks, centered on the interaction between agents and environments. The environment is abstracted into a state space, while agent actions are defined as an action space. Actions are evaluated based on reward functions. A deep Q-Network (DQN) replaces traditional Q-learning's Q-table to address the dimensionality disaster problem caused by expanding state and action spaces. DQN estimates the Q-values for each action, selects the optimal action, and updates network weights accordingly.
3  
★  
State space: abandoning a single CQI value, adopting the CQI historical sequence (length of CHL) to define states. It can perceive the trend of channel changes and solve the adaptation problems caused by high time-variability of channels and feedback delay. The optimal CHL is determined through simulation to be 8.
★  
Action Space: Combining 15 standard MCS with 5 transmission power levels, forming a two-dimensional action set. The MCS includes different modulation orders and encoding rates, with the power level corresponding to specific power values ranging from 0.6 to 1.4. The intelligent body can adjust the modulation order, encoding rate, and transmission power simultaneously.
★  
Reward function: Centered on the energy efficiency of time slots, a reward is given for successful transmission (energy efficiency / consumed power), and a penalty of -1 is imposed for unsuccessful transmissions. This encourages agents to select high-energy-efficiency link parameters.
★  
4  
Simulation Setup: Based on the urban macro-cellular channel model, set subcarrier spacing at 30KHz, time slot duration at 0.5ms, and 52 resource blocks as key parameters for 5G NR. Disable hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ). Use the QL-AMC algorithm based on RL and the standard table-based MCS selection method as benchmarks to evaluate performance in terms of convergence, throughput, BLER, energy efficiency, etc.
Conclusion This paper proposes an intelligent high-efficiency link adaptive algorithm that achieves joint decision-making between transmission power level and modulation coding scheme (MCS). The algorithm introduces a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agent, using CQI historical sequences as states and the combination of MCS and transmission power as actions. It combines historical transmission information to match channel conditions and link parameters (modulation order, encoding rate, transmission power), achieving dynamic adaptation of link parameters. Simulation results show that this algorithm can flexibly adjust link parameters according to channel conditions, improving both energy efficiency and link throughput simultaneously, addressing the issue of traditional methods which only optimize one parameter and have poor adaptability.
Future research can further incorporate more link parameters such as subcarrier spacing and system MIMO order into link adaptation methods, while also considering the quality of service (QoS) requirements for user equipment, to make the link adaptation algorithms more aligned with the complex application scenarios of future 5G and beyond.
